1 · The opportunity

Why social content first

Volume

Every message ships in four or more formats (4:5 feed, 1:1, 1.91:1 link card, 9:16 story) and often as a carousel, for product, brand, regional and recruiting teams. It's the highest-volume content type the team makes.

Where the time goes

Most of the hours aren't spent on ideas. They go on adaptation: resizing, re-cropping, re-setting type, re-checking contrast and copy for each format, then fixing whatever slipped through.

A system worth encoding

Sonderline's guide is precise: a fixed grid, five colours, vermilion as a ~3% signal, 4.5:1 contrast and supplied logos with h/2 clearspace. Those rules are easy to drift from by hand and ideal to enforce in code.

Risk

Pulse is pre-launch. The guide is explicit: "Do not invent capabilities, performance numbers or testimonials." Pre-launch, many hands and fast turnaround make claim drift the biggest brand risk in social.

2 · How the system works

Seven steps, one source of truth

AI works where judgment about language and choice helps. Code handles everything that must be exact. People own the decisions that need taste or accountability. Every step reads the same brand rules, so nothing is hard-coded twice.

01

Brief

Requester states the goal, audience and channels.

Proto: Studio brief panel
Prod: Asana / Jira / Airtable form

02

Draft spec

Claude picks approved lines, layout, photo, crop and formats, and drafts the caption. Gaps get flagged, never invented.

Claude API (Opus 5.5), structured JSON output

03

Compose

Engine renders every format from the spec: grid, type fitting, balanced line breaks, contrast-solving scrim.

Canvas renderer, in the browser + headless Chromium

04

Check

13 guide rules checked on geometry and real pixels. Pass, review (names who decides) or fail (blocked).

Validator, same code everywhere

05

Review

Designer, brand lead, plus product marketing when copy is new. Overrides stay possible: crop, layout, line choice.

Proto: Studio gates
Prod: Figma / Frame.io

06

Deliver

PNG set, caption, alt text, spec, report and approval record travel together.

Proto: zip export
Prod: DAM → scheduler

07

Learn

Approved new lines join the library. Frequent overrides become new layout rules.

4 · Human judgment and quality control

The checker catches what's measurable. People decide the rest.

Checked automatically on every output

Approved on-image copy (p13) · measured text contrast ≥ 4.5:1 (p06) · approved colour pairings (p06) · brand palette only (p03) · vermilion ≤ 5% of pixels (p04) · Inter Tight 400/700 (p05) · fit and collisions (p07) · minimum type size (p05) · supplied logo, undistorted, clearspace kept (p02) · black footer (p07) · story safe zones · photo provenance and enlargement (p08) · assumed assets flagged · caption claims and alt text (p13).

What I would refine before delivery: one request, three versions

A regional team asked for a post "leading with escalations" and wrote in the brief that "Pulse cuts escalation response times by 40%." See it on the deliverables page.

  1. v1, first draft: blocked. White headline on bright metal measured 1.39:1. The caption contains an unapproved performance number. The lead "Resolve escalations faster across every store" isn't in the library, so it's routed to product marketing. Nobody can approve it yet.
  2. v2, auto-fixed: passes, but I wouldn't ship it. Product marketing swaps in approved copy, and the engine solves a 50% scrim to clear contrast. Every check passes, but the metal goes flat and grey. Passing the checks isn't the same as good work.
  3. v3, designer refined: approved. The designer re-crops each format so the photo's own shadow sits behind the headline: 30% scrim on the feed post, none on the link card. The texture comes back and the type reads cleanly. The designer makes the call; the brand lead approves. Product marketing signed off the copy change in v2.

That's the division of labour: the system makes the failure impossible to miss and the fix cheap, and the craft decision stays with a person.

Limitations and failure points

  • Photography is the bottleneck. Two supplied photos limit variety. A real library needs tagging (mode, safe crops, dark zones). Generated imagery would need its own rules for likeness, provenance and disclosure.
  • Implied claims. "Faster" or "smarter" contains no number, so patterns can't block it. The system catches it because it's new copy, and a person decides. That gate must stay.
  • Drafting can be wrong or weak. Claude may choose a flat line or a poor crop. The engine and checks contain the damage, and the designer overrides. If the API is down, the offline drafter and hand-edited specs keep work moving.
  • Measured isn't perceived. Contrast is measured on the pixels under each text block (98th percentile). Busy textures can still feel worse than they measure, and that's a designer call.
  • Template rigidity. New campaign looks need new layout families. That's roughly 1–2 days for a designer and creative technologist each, then they're reusable forever.
  • Prototype shortcuts. The API key is in the browser, approvals are typed names rather than SSO, and the zip stands in for a DAM.

6 · Introducing it to the team

Pilot small, keep designers in charge

Weeks 1–2 · Baseline

Time 20 real social requests end to end. Confirm the copy library with product marketing and the rules with the brand lead. Fill the gaps, such as the flow graphics.

Weeks 3–6 · Pilot

One social pod uses the Studio for real requests. Designers keep full override. Review the overrides weekly: each repeated override becomes a rule or a new layout.

Weeks 7–12 · Scale

Connect intake and the DAM, then add presentations and short video on the same engine and rules. Train the wider team in short sessions using their own briefs.

Ownership

The brand lead owns rules and library. Product marketing owns claims. A creative technologist maintains layouts and the engine. Overrides and failures are reviewed monthly.

7 · Expected impact and limits

Capacity, consistency, fewer review rounds

Time per post set (assumption)

Manual: about 3–5 hours to lay out four formats, resize, chase copy and run QA. With the system: about 30–60 minutes to brief, review and approve.

At volume (assumption)

At 50 post sets a month, roughly 120–200 designer hours a month move from adaptation to concept work. That's capacity, not headcount.

Quality

Off-palette colour, low contrast, stretched logos and unapproved claims can't reach review unflagged. Reviewers spend their time on judgment, not policing.

How we'd prove it

Baseline first, then measure: time to publish, review rounds per asset, override rate and check failure rate. These numbers are estimates until Sonderline's own baseline replaces them.

8 · Assumptions and sources

Assumptions

  • Brand Guide 8.0 is the source of truth. Where the kit's notes differ (e.g. a mockup note describing a full vermilion panel), the guide wins.
  • Flow graphics were listed in the brief but not supplied. I made a clearly labelled stand-in from the S symbol's slanted bars in the guide's colourways. The checker routes any output that uses it to the brand lead.
  • Approved copy = guide page 13 plus the lines used in the guide's own examples.
  • Format sizes and story safe zones (top 250px, bottom 340px) follow current platform guidance.
  • Volumes and hours are illustrative until baselined.
